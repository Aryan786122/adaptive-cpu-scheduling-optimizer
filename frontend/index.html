The Fundamental Operating Systems Trade-Off Explained

Gain Why Did Interactive Latency Improve?

Under non-preemptive FCFS, interactive tasks entering the ready queue must wait for the entire multi-second background job to finish. With bounded cooperative preemption checkpoints (~15ms), the background job safely yields CPU access within milliseconds of an interactive arrival, slashing response time by over 90%.

Cost What Was the Trade-Off Cost?

Background tasks experienced a small completion delay (~2–5% makespan overhead). This minor penalty arises from context switching and yielding execution to prioritize the short burst tasks, illustrating the classic time-sharing scheduling trade-off.

Starvation Prevention How Did Dynamic Aging Prevent Starvation?

If interactive requests keep arriving continuously, pure priority scheduling would starve the background tasks forever. Our scheduler dynamically elevates the effective priority of waiting background jobs over time, ensuring every task eventually receives CPU execution.
